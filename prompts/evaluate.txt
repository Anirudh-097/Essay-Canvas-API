IMPORTANT CONTEXT:
A typical GRE Issue essay is approximately 450–600 words. A single paragraph is only one component of that essay. Evaluate whether this paragraph has an appropriate length and level of development for its role.

Expected paragraph characteristics:
- Introduction: Usually 60–100 words. Should introduce the issue, establish a clear position, and preview the argument. Deduct points for generic openings, unclear thesis, or excessive background.
- Primary argument: Usually 100–180 words. Should contain a focused claim, reasoning explaining why the claim is true, and at least one developed example or meaningful support. A short paragraph with only assertions cannot score highly.
- Counter-argument: Usually 100–180 words. Should accurately present an opposing view, explain its reasoning, and ideally refute or qualify it. A weak or exaggerated counterargument should be penalized.
- Conclusion: Usually 50–100 words. Should synthesize the argument rather than merely repeat the thesis. Deduct points for introducing new ideas or simple repetition.

STRICT SCORING RULES:
Use the full 1–6 scale, but be conservative.

Score calibration:
6:
- Exceptional paragraph.
- Sophisticated reasoning, precise language, strong organization, and effective development.
- Few or no weaknesses.
- Do not award 6 for grammatical correctness alone.

5:
- Strong paragraph with clear reasoning and effective support.
- Minor weaknesses only.
- Ideas are developed beyond surface-level claims.

4:
- Adequate but limited.
- Understandable argument but contains noticeable weaknesses such as generic reasoning, underdeveloped examples, repetition, weak transitions, or insufficient depth.
- A grammatically correct paragraph with shallow reasoning usually belongs here or lower.

3:
- Clearly below GRE expectations.
- Major weaknesses in development, logic, organization, relevance, or paragraph structure.
- Contains mostly assertions without explanation or support.

2:
- Serious problems.
- Minimal development, unclear reasoning, poor coherence, or failure to fulfill paragraph purpose.

1:
- Extremely limited response.
- Does not meaningfully address the assigned role.

MANDATORY DEDUCTIONS:
Apply these rules strictly:

- If a primary argument paragraph lacks a specific example, detailed reasoning, or explanation of consequences, it cannot score above 4.

- If a paragraph consists mainly of claims without explaining "why" or "how," score it 3 or below.
- If the paragraph uses generic statements that could apply to almost any essay topic, deduct points.
- If the paragraph repeats its thesis without adding analysis, deduct points.
- Correct grammar alone must never justify a score above 4.
- A short, simple, logically understandable paragraph should usually score 3, not 4.
